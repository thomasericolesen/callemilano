# Editorial rules and approval register

**Status:** Recommendations reviewed; awaiting human editor-of-record approval.  
**Scope:** Editorial conventions needed to apply `AGENTS.md` and `docs/place-schema.md` consistently before large-scale content generation.  
**Related material:** `docs/project-state.md`, `docs/pilot-review.md`, and `docs/multi-agent-workflow.md`.

This document turns the previously open decisions into recommended rules. These are recommendations, not approved policy: `AGENTS.md` reserves final approval to the human editor-of-record. Apply the current project instructions while approval is pending. Any accepted change to the schema or controlled vocabulary must be recorded in `docs/change-log.md` and applied to related guidance and records together.

## Reviewed recommendations awaiting approval

### 1. Place identity, scope, and representative point

1. **Record scope:** Use one record for one independently identifiable visitor destination with a coherent core experience. Use a venue/attraction record for a bounded site or operator, a route record for a defined route, and a town/city record only when the municipality itself is the recommended destination. Split multi-site destinations when sites have distinct entrances, operators, visitor information, or material access/cost differences.
2. **Representative point:** Use the official public visitor entrance for a bounded venue or attraction; the official start/visitor access point for a route; and the principal public square or civic visitor center for a town. Use `representative_point` only when none of those applies. Label the point and its purpose. Preserve the supplied Maps URL and identity separately; do not silently substitute a different pin.
3. **Claim boundary:** A record describes its named core experience/site. A town record may describe the town-wide visitor experience, but must label town-wide facilities and avoid implying that they are at the representative coordinate. Include nearby amenities only with a named location and verified distance/proximity; otherwise omit them. Do not generalize access conditions from one site to an entire municipality.
4. **Identity threshold:** Resolve identity only when the supplied name/context and at least one authoritative or reliable identity source point to the same destination, and the selected pin is consistent with that identity. For ambiguous or multi-site inputs, retain candidate matches and block progression pending clarification; do not infer identity from name similarity alone.

### 2. Source and evidence standards

1. **Source hierarchy:** Prefer the source responsible for the fact: official operator/venue for its own facilities, hours, restrictions and prices; government/park/heritage authority for public sites and regulations; official transport provider for routes/schedules; official tourism bodies for destination identity and general visitor descriptions. Use reputable secondary sources only when the responsible official source is absent or insufficient, and record that limitation. Maps may establish submitted identity, pin, coordinates, or source URL only; they cannot support safety, access, price, facilities, opening, or suitability claims.
2. **Evidence threshold:** Every factual claim in structured visitor fields or descriptions must have a ledger entry that directly supports that claim. One current, authoritative source is sufficient for ordinary facts. Use a second independent authoritative source or mark the claim unresolved when the claim is safety-critical, disputed, materially ambiguous, or contradicted. A source that only supports destination identity does not support unrelated amenities or recommendations.
3. **Editorial estimates:** Keep estimates identifiable as editorial judgments, document the basis and relevant evidence, qualify them in copy, and never phrase them as operator facts. Do not generate family ratings, accessibility conclusions, or safety guidance without the human review required by current policy. Unsupported claims remain unknown with an issue.
4. **Maps use:** Use Maps only for the submitted identity/pin and coordinates when the pin is clearly the intended point. Prefer an official source for final representative coordinates where available. If the Maps pin conflicts with the official point, preserve the input URL and record the selected official point and rationale.
5. **Evidence ownership:** The `evidence` ledger is the claim-to-source record of record. A field-level `source`, link, or coordinate source may repeat a URL for operational convenience but must not replace its ledger entry. Reuse one evidence entry across fields only when its claim explicitly names every supported field/claim.

### 3. Tags, category boundaries, and seasonality

1. **Tag inclusion:** Apply a tag only when it describes a material part of the named core experience, not merely something present somewhere in the surrounding municipality. `Nature` means the natural feature or activity is a central reason to visit; landscape context alone does not qualify. `Museum` means a museum is the core destination or a named, material part of the record's defined experience. `Viewpoint` requires a specific, accessible-to-visitors viewpoint that is a meaningful draw, not incidental scenery. `Restaurant` means the record itself is a restaurant/café or the core destination includes a documented food experience; nearby dining does not qualify. `Adventure`, `Rainy day`, `Beach`, and `Historic town` require the described activity/use or historic/coastal character to be a material part of the core experience and supported by evidence.
2. **Price-position tags:** `Free` means the defined core experience has no admission charge; optional costs must be stated separately. `Premium` means the core experience is positioned/priced as a premium paid offering, supported by current price/offer evidence; it is not a synonym for expensive or a duplicate of any single `cost_level`. If no clear user-facing value is served, omit either tag. (This interpretation must be checked against the exact controlled vocabulary before adoption; do not create additional tags.)
3. **Category tie-breaker:** Classify by the primary reason a visitor selects the named destination: food venue → `Restaurants`; bathing coastline → `Beaches`; organized or destination-led trip/route → `Excursions`; natural feature/site → `Nature`; municipality as destination → `City`; activity-centered attraction → `Family activities`. For overlap, choose the most specific applicable category and use tags for secondary attributes. Do not alter category enums.
4. **Duration tags:** `Half day` means a typical core visit of more than two and up to five hours; `Full day` means more than five hours and up to one visiting day. These describe time at the destination, excluding travel to/from it. Use the tag only when a supported typical duration is recorded; derive it from `visit_information.typical_duration`. If duration is unknown or straddles a boundary, omit the tag pending review. These proposed cutoffs require editor approval.
5. **Seasonality:** `seasonality` means seasons in which a visit is generally suitable, considering climate and the core experience; it does not mean the venue is open. Record operating schedules/closures in `visit_information` with dated evidence. Use `All year` only where the core experience is reasonably suitable in each season and no material seasonal limitation changes that judgment. Do not add season names as tags; they are not in the controlled tag enum.

### 4. Audience, suitability, energy, and family scores

1. **Age groups:** Include an age group when the core experience is broadly suitable for that audience based on access, restrictions, activity demands, and available experience. Ticket price bands alone never establish suitability. If available evidence cannot support a group-level judgment, omit it and add an issue; do not treat an empty array as “all ages.”
2. **Family score anchors:** `high` = strong fit for the audience across the core experience with no material barrier identified; `medium` = generally workable with a meaningful limitation or adaptation; `low` = substantial mismatch or barrier for that audience; `unknown` = insufficient evidence to judge. A score is suitability guidance, not safety advice or a guarantee. Explain material limitations in the record.
3. **Human review:** Family scores and age-group suitability require human review before editorial review can be considered complete. Automation may suggest candidate values in drafts only, with rationale and evidence; it cannot finalize the values. Accessibility conclusions continue to require human review under `AGENTS.md`.
4. **Energy:** Rate physical effort for the core experience: `Low` for minimal walking/effort on ordinary visitor access; `Medium` for some sustained walking/activity, stairs, gradients, or uneven ground; `High` for sustained or challenging exertion. Base this on sourced route/terrain/activity facts and state material limitations. Energy measures effort, not time spent or excitement; a long, low-effort visit is not automatically `High`.
5. **Safety boundary:** Report sourced restrictions and access facts separately from editorial suitability advice. Never imply an activity is safe for a group solely from a family score, age group, or energy label. Formal age, supervision, or operator restrictions take precedence and must be reported accurately.

### 5. Cost and visit duration

1. **Core cost basis:** Classify the cost of the defined core experience per person. For a town/public realm record, the core experience is access to the public town/space itself; paid attractions, meals, parking, and transport are optional and described separately. For ticketed sites, use standard adult admission for the ordinary core visit, excluding optional extras. Keep the basis and check date in `visit_information`.
2. **Variable prices:** Use the currently published standard price for the selected basis. For dynamic, seasonal, bundled, or “from” prices, state the basis and date, avoid implying a guaranteed total, and use `Unknown` when no stable representative basis can be established. Do not derive a price tier from child tickets or promotions. Optional parking, transport, meals, and equipment remain separate.
3. **Price evidence and freshness:** Use the operator or responsible authority's current price source and record `cost_checked_at`. Recheck price at each editorial review and before approval/publication; if the source is unavailable or materially ambiguous, retain `Unknown` and an issue.
4. **Typical duration:** Record a range when evidence or ordinary variation supports one; otherwise use a clearly qualified point estimate. It covers time at the named core experience, excluding travel, queues, meals, and optional add-ons unless those are integral to the experience. Cite an official suggested duration when published; otherwise mark the value as an editorial estimate with its basis and human review. Duration tags follow the rule in section 3.

### 6. Routing, coordinates, access, and facilities

1. **Driving time:** Use a named route-planning service in driving mode from the fixed origin in `AGENTS.md` to the record's named representative point. Record provider/link and check date. Use the normal recommended route without live traffic where selectable; state when the provider only supplies traffic-aware estimates. Round to the nearest five minutes below one hour and to the nearest ten minutes above one hour; use a range only when route alternatives materially change the estimate. Report one-way time, not distance. Do not claim a stable travel time or infer it from memory.
2. **Coordinate quality:** Prefer an official destination map or source for the representative point. Check that coordinates land on the labeled visitor point, not merely within a large property or municipality. Record purpose, label, source, and date. If the official point cannot be established, use a clearly identified map pin only when identity is unambiguous; otherwise leave unresolved with an issue.
3. **Access/facility scope:** Tie each access or amenity claim to the exact entrance, site, route, or area it covers. Use official operator/authority evidence where available; use `unknown` when the claim is not verifiable. For “nearby,” name the facility/location and record a verified distance or clearly bounded proximity. Do not infer from photographs, generic venue type, or a nearby business listing.
4. **Wheelchair summary mapping:** Derive `yes` only when the core visitor route and relevant essential facilities are documented as wheelchair accessible; `no` when the core route is documented as not wheelchair accessible; `partial` when some but not all material portions/routes/facilities are accessible or access is materially conditional; `unknown` when evidence is missing or too incomplete. Scope-specific access must be explained. This rule must be reconciled with the schema's field definitions before approval.

### 7. Verification dates and maintenance

1. **`last_verified`:** Define it as the date the record's material factual claims were checked as a whole, not the latest check of any one field. Partial checks update their individual evidence `checked_at` values but do not advance `last_verified` until the record-level fact-check pass is complete. This recommended meaning conflicts with the example's note about `last_verified` and must be reconciled in the schema before adoption.
2. **Field freshness:** Use each evidence entry's `checked_at` as the claim-level date. Avoid adding schema fields if this ledger can provide the needed traceability; if a validator cannot reliably map a volatile field to evidence, propose a schema change before scaling rather than maintaining duplicate dates manually.
3. **Review intervals:** Proposed maximum intervals: volatile schedules, prices, booking, parking, access, transport, and facility policies: 90 days; seasonal details: before each relevant season or within 180 days; stable identity, geography, and administrative facts: 24 months. Recheck immediately when a credible correction or source notice appears. These are proposed operating limits, not verified project policy.
4. **Staleness and issue closure:** When a publication-relevant fact exceeds its interval, create a review issue and set the record to `needs_fact_check` before representing the stale fact as current. A partial recheck closes only the issues it directly resolves; it does not mark the whole record verified. Close an issue only with recorded evidence or an explicit editor-of-record decision to retain an honest unknown.

### 8. Descriptions, language, and SEO

1. **Voice/localization:** Use clear international English, concise and practical family-oriented language, and official local spellings/diacritics for names. Avoid slang, hype, first-person experience, review language, and assumptions about family composition. Use metric units and euros for Spain unless a source requires a quoted alternative.
2. **Fact/editorial boundary:** Directly support factual claims in the evidence ledger. Label judgments as recommendations or estimates, qualify uncertainty, and include limitations that materially affect planning. Do not fabricate experience, reviews, amenities, prices, access, or suitability.
3. **Description purpose:** `description_short` is a one- or two-sentence identification and reason to consider the core experience. `description_long` adds verified context, practical limitations, and audience-relevant details without repeating structured fields as a second data source. Avoid boilerplate caveats unless a specific unresolved fact affects planning. Approximate length targets: short 25–45 words; long 80–160 words. These targets are editorial guidance, not schema constraints.
4. **SEO title:** Use canonical place/venue name plus province (or an established brand/location qualifier when needed) and at most one verified distinguishing feature. Target 50–60 characters where practical; preserve clarity and do not truncate names unnaturally. Titles must be unique across the catalog. Recognizable official brands may omit a redundant descriptor, not location when ambiguity remains.
5. **SEO description:** Write a unique, useful summary of the core experience and one verified planning detail or value. Target 140–160 characters where practical, with no keyword stuffing or generic warning. Mention family relevance only when supported by the record; caveats appear only when a material uncertainty affects planning. No mandatory call to action.
6. **Slugs:** Use lowercase ASCII kebab-case derived from the canonical name, transliterating accents consistently (`ñ` → `n`, accented vowels → unaccented vowel). Keep a stable existing slug when the name changes unless clarity or collision requires a rename; add the former slug to `redirects`. This transliteration convention needs approval because the schema does not specify it.

### 9. Unknowns, issue severity, and review gates

1. **Blocking fields:** Identity, representative point, administrative location, material visitor restrictions, current core price when price is asserted, current opening/booking requirements when the record says they apply, safety-relevant access restrictions, and evidence for material factual copy are blocking for approval. Unknowns may remain only where non-blocking and displayed honestly. The editor-of-record may identify additional blocking facts for a destination.
2. **Severity:** `blocking` prevents the next lifecycle transition or approval; `warning` describes a limitation that can proceed if represented honestly; `info` records context/action with no transition impact. Use stable snake_case issue codes and include affected fields, retryability, and owner as required by the workflow.
3. **Status transitions:** A record may move from fact check to editorial review only when identity and core factual claims are supported, required evidence is logged, and remaining unknowns are explicitly non-blocking. Any unresolved blocking issue keeps it in a draft/review state. Approval still requires successful validation, complete evidence for asserted claims, no blocking issues, and the human decision.
4. **Scale controls:** Bulk generation may create drafts only. Before a batch proceeds beyond intake, validate a representative sample of draft structure and review all identity ambiguities; before any record enters human approval, validate that record individually. No sampling substitutes for per-record identity resolution, evidence, validation, or human approval. Agents never approve or publish.

## Existing constraints that are not open for local reinterpretation

- Canonical place data lives only in Markdown front matter under `docs/places/`; the catalog is generated, not hand-edited.
- The primary categories, tags, age groups, family-score keys, energy labels, season labels, cost labels, and lifecycle statuses are the exact controlled values in `AGENTS.md` and `docs/place-schema.md`. No new values or synonyms may be introduced without editor approval and schema change.
- The driving origin is fixed as Casa de la Familia, Urbanización Cerros del Águila, Las Lagunas de Mijas, Málaga, Spain. Route time is one-way, approximate, sourced, and dated; it must not be guessed.
- Claims require appropriate evidence. Unknowns and review issues are preferable to unsupported facts.
- Stable IDs come from the central registry. Slug changes require redirects. Image reuse requires documented rights and approved status.
- Only the human editor-of-record may approve, publish, archive, or change schema/taxonomy. Agents prepare drafts; validation does not replace human approval.
