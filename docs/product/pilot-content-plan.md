# 50-destination pilot content plan

**Audience:** Guests staying at Casa de la Familia in Cerros del Águila.  
**Purpose:** Launch a useful, trustworthy activity guide with 50 distinct destinations. Fifty is the target size, not a reason to lower the evidence or review standard.

## Category sequence and target mix

Include all six approved categories at launch so guests can choose across different kinds of days. Begin research with places that are easy to compare for a family outing, then expand into longer or more complex plans. Counts below are planning targets; replace a weak candidate with a stronger one rather than publishing it to fill a quota.

| Category | Target at launch | Content sequence |
|---|---:|---|
| `Restaurants` | 6 | Include early for practical meal planning, favoring places with reliable official menus, location, and current visitor information. Restaurant details change often, so avoid padding the count with weakly sourced listings. |
| `Beaches` | 10 | Prioritize early. Include a useful range of beach experiences and clearly explain access, parking, facilities, and relevant seasonal conditions. |
| `Excursions` | 8 | Include a few in the first wave, then expand. These often need stronger travel, booking, duration, and restriction checks. |
| `Nature` | 8 | Prioritize early, with varied effort levels and clear route/access limitations. |
| `City` | 8 | Include town and city destinations where the visitor point and scope can be explained clearly; do not imply one pin represents every attraction. |
| `Family activities` | 10 | Prioritize early. Verify age/height restrictions, booking, price, duration, and access before recommending activity venues. |
| **Total** | **50** | |

**Wave 1: first 30 candidates** — 8 `Beaches`, 8 `Family activities`, 4 `Nature`, 4 `City`, 3 `Restaurants`, and 3 `Excursions`. This establishes immediate options across short outings, family activities, nature, and practical meals while testing the review process.

**Wave 2: next 20 candidates** — 2 `Beaches`, 2 `Family activities`, 4 `Nature`, 4 `City`, 3 `Restaurants`, and 5 `Excursions`. Use these to fill genuine coverage gaps and add more day-trip choices.

Screen more than 50 candidates to allow for duplicates, ambiguous place scope, and destinations that cannot meet the quality bar. Do not compensate for an empty category by changing category labels or including unsuitable records.

## Minimum quality requirements

Every destination recommended publicly must:

- Be a distinct, correctly identified place with a clear explanation of what a guest visits and one labeled visitor point that matches its directions.
- Have a useful, family-focused description based on supported facts. Distinguish facts from editorial guidance and do not invent facilities, experience, or suitability.
- Include a dated, sourced one-way drive estimate from Casa de la Familia to the named visitor point and a realistic typical time at the destination. These are separate commitments.
- Give guests a reliable way to confirm current opening and booking information, and accurately state known requirements, prices, and material extra costs.
- Explain age or participation restrictions, physical effort, and material accessibility limitations. Family suitability and accessibility must receive human review; do not infer them from a venue type or photograph.
- Identify essential arrival and visit details when relevant: parking and walk to entrance, toilets, food, transport, and dog policy. Distinguish on-site from nearby services.
- Use current official venue, government, tourism, transport, park, or heritage sources where available. Every material factual claim must have claim-specific source evidence, a check date, and a checker. Maps may help establish identity and coordinates, but not visitor claims.
- Retain unknowns and conflicts as review issues during drafting. Do not publish a material unknown as if it were confirmed; escalate conflicting or high-impact claims to the editor-of-record.
- Use the approved controlled categories and labels, stable registered IDs, unique slugs, and permitted image assets. Images are optional; no image is better than an asset with unclear rights.

## Publication checklist

Complete this checklist for each candidate before human approval:

1. **Identity and scope:** Confirm the exact destination, duplicate status, public name, administrative location, record scope, and representative visitor point.
2. **Useful guest answer:** Confirm the page explains the core experience, audience fit, effort, duration, travel time, and material limitations clearly.
3. **Current practical facts:** Check date-specific opening, seasonal operation where relevant, prices and basis, booking rules, restrictions, access, parking, and facilities against appropriate sources.
4. **Plan readiness:** Confirm the directions and drive estimate use the same point; explain material required costs and whether booking is needed; link to the responsible official source for details that can change.
5. **Evidence and dates:** Ensure each factual claim has direct evidence with source URL, source type, date checked, checker, and confidence. Make unresolved uncertainty visible and correctly classified.
6. **Editorial review:** Check that family suitability, age guidance, and accessibility judgments are qualified and human-reviewed; ensure the description is concise, useful, and free of unsupported claims.
7. **Content and rights validation:** Check required record content, allowed values, URLs, ID/slug uniqueness, duplicate identity, evidence coverage, review issues, catalog/SEO uniqueness, and image-rights approval for any included asset.
8. **Lifecycle and sign-off:** Keep failed or incomplete records in draft/review status. A named validator records the outcome; the human editor-of-record records approval. Publication follows the separate publication process.

## Review workflow

1. **Prepare intake:** Gather candidates, record their source/context, identify duplicates, and reserve IDs through the central registry. Keep intake progress per destination.
2. **Resolve identity:** Confirm the exact place and intended visitor point. Put ambiguous names, town-wide scope, or conflicting pins on hold for clarification.
3. **Fact-check:** Verify destination identity, route, schedule, price, booking, restrictions, access, parking, transport, and facilities as applicable. Research independent claims separately, then consolidate evidence and unresolved issues.
4. **Edit for guest decisions:** Explain the actual experience and who it may suit. Put practical limits where guests make the choice; do not fill gaps with generic promotional copy.
5. **Prepare browse content:** Set approved category/labels and unique, accurate page and search copy. Confirm the listing will help guests compare time, travel, cost, effort, and fit.
6. **Validate and hand off:** Run the mandatory validation gate; until automation exists, a named reviewer completes and records the same checklist manually. Return failures to the responsible stage.
7. **Human approval and publication:** The editor-of-record resolves source disputes and review decisions, approves eligible records, and then uses the separate publication process. Agents prepare drafts only and never approve or publish.
8. **Maintain:** Assign review dates according to approved volatility intervals. Recheck changing hours, prices, booking, access, parking, transport, and facilities; route corrections through the queue and return material factual changes to fact check.

Resolve pending editorial rules that affect scope, tags, suitability, cost, duration, freshness, and SEO before large-scale generation. Until then, avoid treating pilot conventions as approved policy.

## Acceptance criteria

The 50-destination public pilot is ready when:

- It contains 50 distinct destinations, all in the six approved categories, with the target mix used as a coverage guide rather than a reason to lower standards.
- Every public recommendation passes the individual publication checklist and validation gate, has complete evidence for material claims, no unresolved blocking issue, and an approval decision from the human editor-of-record.
- A guest can compare options by activity, likely time away, party fit, effort, and cost; identify the correct visitor point; and confirm current hours or booking with a responsible source.
- Every important unknown is clearly disclosed, and no unsupported claim is presented as fact. Candidates that cannot meet the bar remain unpublished.
- The collection offers useful variety across short outings, full-day plans, indoor/outdoor options where evidence supports them, and different effort levels. The mix is based on verified usefulness, not just category totals.
- A review owner and next review date are set for every published destination, with a correction route available to guests and the editorial team.

The launch succeeds when guests can make and carry out better-informed choices—not when the guide merely reaches 50 pages.
