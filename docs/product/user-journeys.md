# Guest journeys

**Audience:** Guests staying at Casa de la Familia in Cerros del Águila. These are representative planning situations, not claims about any particular guest party. Each journey should help guests make a choice and know what to check before leaving.

## 1. Fit an outing into a short day

- **Situation:** Guests have a free morning or afternoon and want to get out without committing the whole day.
- **Constraints:** Limited time at the destination, drive from the house, meal/rest plans, and the need to return on time.
- **Information needed:** One-way drive estimate to the exact visitor point; typical time at the destination; opening hours; booking requirement; whether a shorter visit still makes sense.
- **Filters used:** Short visit, lower travel time, open on the chosen date, no advance booking or confirmed availability.
- **Decision reached:** Choose a nearby-enough, open destination whose visit and drive fit the available window; otherwise save it for a full day.
- **Success criteria:** Guests can estimate total time away from the house, confirm the destination is open, and avoid a plan that depends on rushing.

## 2. Plan a full-day outing

- **Situation:** Guests are willing to spend most of the day away for a destination that justifies the travel.
- **Constraints:** Door-to-door travel, opening schedule, meal and toilet breaks, ticket costs, and a reasonable return time.
- **Information needed:** Drive estimate and arrival point; typical visit duration; current hours; booking and entry rules; food/toilets; total likely party cost; parking or transit options.
- **Filters used:** Full-day experience, suitable for the party, open on the chosen date, manageable cost, facilities available, booking confirmed.
- **Decision reached:** Select the destination only if its main experience, current schedule, and logistics justify the travel; otherwise choose a closer alternative.
- **Success criteria:** The party knows when to leave, how long to allow, where to arrive, what to book, and how to handle meals and breaks.

## 3. Choose an activity for a mixed-age family

- **Situation:** Adults are choosing one outing for children in different age groups, with different interests or energy levels.
- **Constraints:** The activity must be worthwhile for the adults and workable for the youngest or least mobile member; formal age, height, or supervision rules may apply.
- **Information needed:** What the visit actually involves; age suitability and its reasons; effort, terrain, restrictions, duration, and practical limitations.
- **Filters used:** Relevant age ranges, low-to-moderate effort where needed, suitable activity type, no unmet restrictions, enough shared interest.
- **Decision reached:** Choose a shared experience when every member can participate meaningfully; otherwise select an alternative or split the day.
- **Success criteria:** No one is surprised by an age/height limit or physical demand, and the group understands who is likely to enjoy which parts.

## 4. Check access for a stroller, wheelchair, or limited mobility

- **Situation:** A guest or child uses a wheelchair or stroller, or a party member needs a manageable route.
- **Constraints:** Steps, gradients, surface, distance, accessible toilets, entrances, and parking-to-entrance route may differ across a large site.
- **Information needed:** Exact entrance and route conditions; step-free or wheelchair route; stroller suitability; relevant facilities; parking location and walking distance; source and recency of the information.
- **Filters used:** Verified accessible route or manageable terrain, appropriate facilities, suitable parking, no unaddressed barrier on the core visit.
- **Decision reached:** Choose the destination only when the route and key facilities work for the person’s needs; if information is incomplete, contact the operator or choose a better-documented option.
- **Success criteria:** The guest can identify the entrance and route they will use and understands any partial access or limitations before setting out.

## 5. Keep the outing within a family budget

- **Situation:** Guests compare outings and want to know the likely cost for their actual party.
- **Constraints:** Adult and child ticket prices may differ; family tickets, parking, transport, meals, and optional activities can add costs.
- **Information needed:** Current prices and age bands; family ticket or discounts; what admission includes; required add-ons; parking/transport costs; date checked.
- **Filters used:** Maximum expected spend, free/low-cost core experience, family ticket availability, limited required extras.
- **Decision reached:** Choose the option whose expected total for the party fits the budget, or choose a lower-cost alternative. Do not treat a generic price tier as a guaranteed total.
- **Success criteria:** Guests can estimate the main unavoidable costs and identify what remains optional or date-dependent.

## 6. Book a date-sensitive visit

- **Situation:** Guests are considering a venue or activity that may require advance reservations or timed entry.
- **Constraints:** Desired date, limited slots, booking lead time, seasonal operation, cancellation terms, and the possibility that plans change.
- **Information needed:** Whether booking is required or recommended; booking link; current date/slot availability; lead time; applicable rules and cancellation terms; current opening calendar.
- **Filters used:** Open on the target date, available time slot, booking still possible, acceptable cancellation conditions.
- **Decision reached:** Reserve only after confirming live availability and the correct date; if unavailable, choose an open alternative without a reservation dependency.
- **Success criteria:** Guests have a valid confirmation or a clear backup, and do not mistake a booking link for proof that a slot is available.

## 7. Choose around heat, rain, or seasonal conditions

- **Situation:** Guests are planning for a hot day, rain, wind, or a season that may alter the experience.
- **Constraints:** Outdoor exposure, shade/shelter, water or trail conditions, seasonal closures, daylight, and the comfort of children or older relatives.
- **Information needed:** Whether the core visit is suitable in that season; current operating status; exposure and shelter; weather-dependent activities; practical caveats and an indoor or sheltered fallback.
- **Filters used:** Suitable in current conditions, open that day, adequate shade/shelter or weather protection, alternative available if conditions change.
- **Decision reached:** Go when conditions suit the activity; switch to a sheltered or rainy-day option when comfort, access, or operations may be affected.
- **Success criteria:** Guests know which conditions could change the plan and can identify a workable alternative before leaving.

## 8. Make the trip without relying on a car

- **Situation:** Guests do not have a car available, prefer not to drive, or want to combine public transport with walking.
- **Constraints:** Current service times, transfers, fares, last-mile distance and effort, return service, and the party’s mobility or stroller needs.
- **Information needed:** Current transport route and schedule; stop/access point; journey duration and cost; walking connection to the entrance; return options.
- **Filters used:** Service on the chosen day; manageable transfers and walking; suitable return time; acceptable total journey time and fare.
- **Decision reached:** Choose a destination with a complete, workable outbound and return plan; otherwise choose a car-accessible or closer alternative.
- **Success criteria:** Guests know how to reach the visitor entrance and return to the house without depending on an unverified service.

## 9. Plan breaks, food, and dietary needs

- **Situation:** The outing is long enough that the party needs toilets, food, or a planned meal break; someone may have dietary needs.
- **Constraints:** Whether facilities are on-site or nearby, their operating hours, children’s food options, allergens, outside-food rules, and accessible toilets.
- **Information needed:** Facility location and hours; what food is offered; official menu/dietary information; whether outside food is allowed; distance to nearby alternatives.
- **Filters used:** On-site toilets and food when needed; suitable menu or verified dietary options; permitted food plan; accessible facilities where required.
- **Decision reached:** Choose the venue if breaks and meals are manageable; otherwise bring permitted food, plan a nearby stop, or select another destination.
- **Success criteria:** Guests know where breaks can happen and are not relying on an unverified assumption about food or toilets.

## 10. Bring a dog or keep a flexible backup plan

- **Situation:** Guests are travelling with a dog, or want a second option in case a venue is closed, unsuitable, or affected by weather.
- **Constraints:** Dog access may vary by area, season, or activity; backup destinations must fit the same time, travel, and party needs.
- **Information needed:** Current dog policy and conditions; a nearby or weather-appropriate alternative; travel time and opening status for the backup.
- **Filters used:** Dog permitted under the party’s conditions; alternative open on the same day; similar duration and effort; manageable additional travel.
- **Decision reached:** Keep the original plan if the policy and conditions fit; otherwise choose the pre-checked alternative rather than making a new decision after arrival.
- **Success criteria:** Guests have a confirmed dog plan or a realistic backup with verified hours and directions, avoiding a wasted drive.

## Journey design principles

- Start with what changes the choice: available time, party needs, travel, cost, access, and date-specific availability.
- Show the exact visitor point and distinguish time spent there from travel time.
- Put material restrictions and unknowns where guests make the decision, not behind generic descriptions.
- Treat live schedules, prices, transport, and booking availability as changeable; link to the responsible source and make the check date clear.
- Offer alternatives when a common constraint rules out the first choice, while keeping recommendations evidence-based.
