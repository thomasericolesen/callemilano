# La Cala Beach (Playa de La Cala)

**A long, sandy town beach with lifeguard cover, beach restaurants and an adapted bathing area in season**  
**Visitor area:** La Cala de Mijas seafront, Mijas, Málaga  
**Last verified:** 4 October 2026

## Quick verdict

La Cala is a convenient nearby choice for a family beach day: Mijas Tourism describes easy access on foot, by car and by bus, and lists fine sand, six beach restaurants, a first-aid post and adapted bathing. The municipality’s 2026 lifeguard schedule runs daily from June through September, and on weekends and public holidays in May and October. Outside those periods, do not assume the beach has lifeguards on duty. [Beach profile](https://turismo.mijas.es/en/nature/beaches/la-cala-beach?print=1&tmpl=component) · [2026 beach status and lifeguard schedule](https://turismo.mijas.es/en/nature/beaches/state-of-the-beaches)

**Best for:** a flexible beach visit with food options nearby and seasonal support for people with reduced mobility.  
**Choose another plan if:** your group needs a quiet or uncrowded beach, or if you need lifeguard/adapted bathing services outside their published operating dates.

## Why visit

Mijas Tourism lists La Cala Beach as **1,258 metres long and 38 metres wide on average**, with fine sand and a sandy seabed. The current municipal profile lists six beach restaurants, 450 hammocks/sun loungers, one first-aid post and easy access by foot, car or bus. La Cala retained its Blue Flag for 2026–2027. [Official beach profile](https://turismo.mijas.es/en/nature/beaches/la-cala-beach?print=1&tmpl=component) · [Blue Flag announcement](https://turismo.mijas.es/en/news/1211-mijas-beaches-renew-their-five-blue-flags)

**Our recommendation:** Plan around the beach access and facilities that suit your family, rather than assuming every service is available all year. In peak season, arrive earlier if you want a particular spot or sun lounger.

## Travel from Casa de la Familia

Allow **about 10–15 minutes by car** from Casa de la Familia in Cerros del Águila to the central La Cala seafront in normal traffic. Allow longer at beach-time traffic peaks. The beach is long, so choose your parking/access point before driving; a different section may add a walk. [Open driving directions to La Cala seafront](https://www.google.com/maps/dir/?api=1&origin=Casa%20de%20la%20Familia%2C%20Urbanizaci%C3%B3n%20Cerros%20del%20%C3%81guila%2C%20Mijas&destination=La%20Cala%20Beach%2C%20La%20Cala%20de%20Mijas%2C%20M%C3%A1laga&travelmode=driving).

Mijas lists bus routes serving La Cala, including lines 119, 220 and 156. Route 156 connects Las Lagunas/Miramar with Cerros del Águila; check the current [municipal transport links](https://turismo.mijas.es/en/basic-information/how-to-get-there) and operator timetable before relying on a service.

## Time, cost and services

- **Recommended visit:** 2–4 hours; make it a half-day if you plan to swim, eat or rent loungers. This is an editorial estimate; there is no admission gate or fixed visit duration.
- **Entry:** The municipal beach profile lists no admission charge. Beach restaurants, loungers and other optional services are separate purchases.
- **Beach facilities:** The municipal profile lists six beach restaurants, 450 loungers and a first-aid post. A municipal beach-services map lists toilets, showers, accessible showers, foot-rinsing points and access paths; the map is older, so check current availability on arrival. [Municipal beach map (PDF)](https://turismo.mijas.es/images/Guias/Senda%20Litoral.pdf)
- **Lifeguards in 2026:** Daily 11:00–20:00 from 1 June to 30 September; 11:00–19:00 on Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays in May and October. The municipality publishes weekend/public-holiday coastal-path patrol arrangements in several winter months, but that is not the same as regular beach lifeguard coverage. Check the [current beach-state page](https://turismo.mijas.es/en/nature/beaches/state-of-the-beaches).
- **Parking and lounger prices:** **Verification required:** current parking charges, availability and sun-lounger rates are not stated in the municipal beach profile. Check local signs or the service operator before committing.

## Access and family suitability

Mijas Tourism identifies La Cala as a beach with adapted bathing for people with reduced mobility. The municipal adapted-bathing information describes a staffed area at La Cala–Torreón, with shade, loungers, crutches, a lift and amphibious chairs. The detailed facility leaflet was last updated in August 2024; use the 2026 beach-status page or call the service before relying on the equipment. The 2024 document gives **951 08 88 98 / 655 90 30 63** for the bathing service. [Adapted bathing information (PDF)](https://turismo.mijas.es/images/playas/INFO%20PLAYAS%20PDF/PLAYAS%20CON%20BA%C3%91O%20ADAPTADO.pdf)

| Group | What to consider |
|---|---|
| Toddlers | Fine sand and nearby food options can make for an easy-going beach visit. Bring shade and check the sea conditions on arrival. |
| Children | Suitable for a flexible swim-and-sand day when conditions are appropriate; supervise closely near the water. |
| Teenagers | Good for swimming and spending time along the seafront; seasonal crowding may matter. |
| Adults | A practical local beach with services and places to eat. |
| Seniors | Easy access is listed by the municipality. The adapted bathing area is seasonal; check the route and service hours for your visit date. |

**Strollers:** The access is described as easy, but sand can make pushing a stroller along the beach difficult. A seafront access path may help; choose the entry point before setting out.  
**Wheelchairs:** Adapted bathing is provided at La Cala–Torreón in season, with specialist equipment and staff. This does not mean every stretch of sand is wheelchair-accessible; confirm the exact service point and operating hours.

## Weather and practical tips

Check the municipality’s live beach-condition symbols on the day; the status can change. Bring sun protection and drinking water, particularly in summer. Lifeguard cover is seasonal, and Blue Flag status is not a same-day swimming-safety report. If bathing conditions are marked unsuitable or prohibited, follow the posted advice.
