# CalleMilano content types

**Audience:** Guests staying at Casa de la Familia in Cerros del Águila.  
**Purpose:** Define the page structure and publication standard for the first public collection. This document describes editorial page types; it does not change the project's approved category vocabulary.

Use one shared decision-making core on every page, then add only the sections that matter for that kind of visit. Label fact-based claims with current evidence; distinguish editorial recommendations from verified facts. If a material fact is unresolved, write **Verification required** during drafting and hold publication when the uncertainty could make the outing unsuitable or waste the trip.

## 1. Attraction template

**Use for:** Ticketed or managed visitor venues and bookable experiences. Use for `Family activities`; use for an `Excursions` record only when the record identifies a specific attraction, tour, or bookable experience. A town-centred day trip should use the Town template.

### Required sections

- Quick verdict: best fit, time commitment, and main caveat.
- What the visitor does/experiences; who may enjoy it and who should choose something else.
- Travel estimate from Casa to the exact entrance/meeting point; typical on-site duration.
- Current admission prices by relevant age/ticket type; a stated example party total and known required add-ons.
- Opening pattern, date-specific schedule link, booking requirement and official booking link.
- Formal participation rules: minimum age/height, supervision, re-entry, timed slots, or other exclusions as relevant.
- Physical effort and access limitations, including separate stroller and wheelchair information when applicable.
- Arrival basics: parking/transport, walk to entrance, toilets, food, and material weather/season caveats.
- Last-checked date and source links for material claims.

### Optional sections

- Activity or show schedule, animal/feature visibility caveats, guided tour options, accessibility contact, indoor/outdoor alternatives, nearby backup, brief background, photos, or a day itinerary.

### Publication blockers

- Identity, entrance, or core experience is ambiguous.
- No checked route estimate or realistic duration.
- Current opening/booking path is unknown or no reliable official live source is linked.
- Price basis, material mandatory extras, age/height/supervision rules, or participation limits are unresolved where applicable.
- A material access barrier or physical demand is unknown and could affect suitability.
- Evidence does not support the guest-facing claims or date-sensitive details.

### Recommended evidence

- Official venue/operator pages for admission, schedules, tickets, access, restrictions, re-entry, facilities, and policies.
- Official access map and venue contact for route-level mobility details.
- Current official menu/service page for venue food; government/transport source for transit; route provider and dated check for the drive estimate.
- Direct written venue confirmation when public information is broad or ambiguous.

### Guest decision questions answered

Is this worth the time and ticket for us? What will we actually do? Can everyone participate? What must we book? What will our party likely pay? How long will we be there? Where do we enter, park, eat, and find essential facilities?

## 2. Town template

**Use for:** A town, historic centre, or city area where the visitor explores more than one street or landmark. The page must define the area it covers; it must not imply that one pin or one opening schedule represents the whole town.

### Required sections

- Quick verdict and a plain-language scope: what area is covered and what is outside it.
- Main experience and reasons to visit; who may enjoy it and who may prefer another outing.
- Named starting/arrival point, practical route or compact walking circuit, dated drive estimate, and suggested time in the area.
- Terrain/effort and material walking, access, stroller, or wheelchair limitations for the described route.
- Parking and public-transport options with last-mile information when verified.
- For featured paid or scheduled landmarks: identify that hours, prices, booking, and access are separate; link to each official source. Do not imply entry is included in a town stroll.
- Relevant toilets, food, and weather/season notes along the stated route, or disclose that they have not been confirmed.
- Last-checked date and claim-specific source links.

### Optional sections

- Landmark shortlist with separate visit estimates, self-guided route/map, market or event calendar, a rainy-day alternative, accessibility-focused route, nearby destinations, and historical context.

### Publication blockers

- The area/scope or suggested arrival point is unclear.
- A town-wide listing is represented by a pin that could send guests to the wrong place.
- The walking circuit, effort, or duration is not clear enough to judge feasibility.
- A material access/terrain limitation is unknown for the route being recommended.
- Featured landmarks are described as open, free, or included without evidence for their individual rules.
- No dated route estimate from Casa to the named starting point.

### Recommended evidence

- Municipal tourism/culture maps and official town walking-route materials.
- Municipal parking, transport, and accessibility information.
- Official venue pages for each landmark with separate hours, admission, and booking details.
- Current municipal event notices for events or markets; dated route-provider check for driving time.

### Guest decision questions answered

What exactly will we visit? Where should we start? How much walking and uphill effort is involved? How long should we allow? Which sights are open or ticketed? Can our party manage the route and arrival?

## 3. Beach template

**Use for:** A named beach or clearly bounded beach access area. Keep the beach distinct from a nearby marina, dune route, or beach club unless the page explicitly covers that combined visit.

### Required sections

- Quick verdict: likely beach-day fit and the most important access or condition caveat.
- Exact beach access point and a clear map/directions link; dated drive estimate to that point.
- Suggested visit commitment, party-fit guidance, and material sand/steps/terrain limitations.
- Current seasonal service/opening context, including which facilities operate only in season.
- Verified access and mobility information, with separate stroller and wheelchair/bathing-access details where relevant.
- Parking and walk to sand/access point; current cost or a clear note that it is unverified.
- Current official source for bathing-water status, weather or beach warnings when available; never promise safe/calmer conditions from general reputation.
- Toilets, showers, food, lifeguard, shade, rentals, and dog rules when material; distinguish on-site from nearby and seasonal from year-round.
- Cost basis for free access and any known paid services; last-checked date and source links.

### Optional sections

- Seasonal bathing assistance, accessible beach facilities, dunes or coastal path route, water-sport operators, public transport, nearby meal option, tide/wind guidance, and a weather backup.

### Publication blockers

- The precise beach or access point is ambiguous, or beach and adjacent natural-area scopes are conflated.
- The drive estimate is not tied to the selected entrance/access point.
- Material access barriers or seasonal closures are unverified.
- Any claim about current water quality, lifeguard presence, swimming conditions, or service availability lacks current authoritative evidence.
- Cost or availability of an essential paid service is presented as certain without current evidence.
- The page implies a whole beach/dune/coastal route is accessible based on one facility or access point.

### Recommended evidence

- Municipal beach authority for beach identity, facilities, lifeguard calendar, access, bathing assistance, and contact details.
- Government/environmental authority for protected-area rules, path access, and current water-quality or warning information.
- Official map for access and parking point; current authority or operator page for rentals, food, or beach services.
- Dated weather/sea-state source where relevant to same-day guidance.

### Guest decision questions answered

Which beach should we navigate to? Can our party get from parking to the sand/water? What is available on this date? What might cost extra? Are current conditions suitable for our planned visit?

## 4. Restaurant template

**Use for:** A restaurant, café, or other meal destination. The main decision is whether the food, meal timing, expected bill, and access fit the guest's party.

### Required sections

- Quick verdict describing the cuisine/meal proposition in plain terms, without promotional claims.
- Exact address, dated drive estimate, and arrival/parking note.
- Current opening and kitchen hours, closed days, and booking method/requirement or verified walk-in guidance.
- Current official menu link with representative prices; give a clearly labeled example bill for a stated party/order, or explain why no reliable total can be estimated.
- Children's menu/options when offered; material dietary/allergen/outside-food policy only when supported.
- Access limitations relevant to the dining experience, including entrance, seating, toilets, and stroller/wheelchair details when known.
- On-site facilities and weather/seating considerations when they affect the plan.
- Last-checked date, contact route, and sources for hours/menu/policy.

### Optional sections

- Sample orders for different budgets, dietary menu links, high-chair information, terrace/view details, nearby parking alternatives, reservation advice for peak periods, and nearby activity pairings.

### Publication blockers

- Current operation, venue identity, address, or visitor entrance is uncertain.
- Current service hours/kitchen periods cannot be established or no reliable live source is linked.
- Price information is absent for a promoted paid meal recommendation, or a family total is presented without clear order assumptions.
- A dietary/allergen/access claim that could materially affect a guest is unsupported.
- Booking is represented as available/required without a current booking route or direct confirmation.
- No dated route estimate from Casa to the restaurant entrance.

### Recommended evidence

- Restaurant's own current website/menu/reservation page and direct contact confirmation for volatile details.
- Official menu with prices, allergen notes, children's options, and outside-food policy.
- Venue accessibility/facility details; municipal parking/transport information for arrival claims.
- Dated route check to the exact entrance.

### Guest decision questions answered

Will the menu suit us? What will a meal roughly cost? When can we eat? Should we reserve? Can everyone enter, sit, and use needed facilities? How do we arrive and park?

## 5. Nature/Hiking template

**Use for:** A trail, viewpoint route, natural area, botanical walk, or other outdoor nature visit. A broad natural area must be narrowed to a usable visitor point and route.

### Required sections

- Quick verdict identifying route character, effort, likely party fit, and principal risk/limitation.
- Exact trailhead/visitor point and route definition: distance, direction (loop/linear), elevation/effort, and realistic time.
- Dated one-way drive estimate to the trailhead; return transport plan for linear routes.
- Terrain, footwear, mobility/stroller limits, age restrictions, and health cautions when authoritative guidance exists.
- Current opening/access status, permits/booking, closures, protected-area rules, and seasonal/weather conditions.
- Parking/transport, toilets, water/food availability, and where facilities are located relative to the route.
- Cost and material required equipment/transport costs.
- Last-checked date and official sources for route conditions and restrictions.

### Optional sections

- Route map/profile, alternative shorter/longer loops, seasonal wildlife/botany notes, shade/exposure, emergency/contact information, nearby backup, and linked public transport timetable.

### Publication blockers

- Trailhead, route, route direction, or finish point is ambiguous.
- Distance/time/effort or return logistics are missing or internally inconsistent.
- Current access/closure status or material permit/restriction information is unknown.
- Safety-, health-, mobility-, or age-related conditions are asserted without authoritative support.
- A linear route has no viable return plan, or essential route facilities are implied without verification.
- No dated route estimate to the named trailhead.

### Recommended evidence

- Park/land manager or official trail authority for route maps, closures, restrictions, access, safety, and facilities.
- Government/topographic route information for distance and elevation, cross-checked against the official route.
- Transport/parking operator for return connections and costs; official weather authority for date-specific alerts.
- Dated route-provider check from Casa to the precise trailhead.

### Guest decision questions answered

Can everyone in our group complete this route? How long, steep, and exposed is it? Where does it start and finish? How do we return? What can close or restrict access? What must we bring, and where are toilets/water?

## Shared core fields

Every destination page, regardless of type, should include or deliberately mark as not applicable:

- **Identity and scope:** Public name, destination type/category as approved, concise description of what the guest will visit, municipality, and any scope boundary.
- **Exact visitor point:** Named entrance, start, meeting point, or representative arrival point with an official directions/map link.
- **Quick verdict:** Best-fit guest/day, main value, time commitment, and most important caveat; fact and editorial judgment are distinguishable.
- **Party fit:** Who may enjoy it, who should consider another option, physical effort, relevant age guidance, and formal restrictions separately.
- **Time commitment:** Dated one-way drive estimate from Casa to the named point and supported typical visit duration; state scope/assumptions.
- **Cost and booking:** Current price basis and material required extras; booking requirement and official route, or a supported “not applicable.” Give a party-cost example when it meaningfully helps.
- **Access and arrival:** Material accessibility limitations, stroller/wheelchair suitability where relevant, parking/transport basics, and any last-mile barrier.
- **Essentials:** Opening/season status, toilets/food or a clear not-applicable/unknown note, plus material weather or operational caveats.
- **Reliable next step:** Official website, live schedule/pricing/booking/contact links as applicable.
- **Freshness and evidence:** Last-checked date for volatile information and claim-supporting sources. Mark unresolved material details **Verification required**.

Shared fields are a minimum decision aid, not a demand to fill irrelevant sections. “Not applicable” should be used only when the reason is clear; “unknown” should not be disguised as “no.”

## Minimum number of content types for launch

Use **five editorial content types**: Attraction, Town, Beach, Restaurant, and Nature/Hiking. Do not add separate types for museums, parks, guided tours, viewpoints, or excursions at launch; assign them to the closest existing type according to the guest's main decision. For example, a ticketed guided activity uses Attraction, while a town-day visit uses Town.

This is the smallest useful set that preserves meaningful differences in blockers and decision information. Build it from a shared core rather than five unrelated page designs. Keep the project's approved category labels unchanged; template choice controls page emphasis, not taxonomy.
